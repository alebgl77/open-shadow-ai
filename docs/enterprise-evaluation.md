# Evaluate Open Shadow AI for an enterprise pilot

Open Shadow AI helps IT and security teams discover AI signals across selected sources, inspect the evidence and record human review. It is open-source, self-hosted and scoped to one organization per deployment. Start with the [15-minute synthetic walkthrough](../README.md#try-the-interface) to see the workflow before connecting any enterprise source.

## What you can evaluate

| Question | Evidence available in the console | Boundary to record |
|---|---|---|
| Where do we have visibility? | The dashboard shows a dated observation window and the last retained event by source type. **Sources & coverage** describes source-specific limits. | Last retained events can fall outside the observation window. Fleet and user coverage depend on the collection scope. Unmanaged endpoints, unobserved encrypted DNS, uncollected directories and embedded SaaS AI can leave gaps. |
| What did we observe? | In **Discoveries**, inspect the source, sample, timestamps and reasoning for a match. | A DNS lookup can come from prefetching. Endpoint evidence establishes reported presence. Neither establishes a completed AI request or data disclosure. Model, token and cost evidence depend on instrumentation. |
| Who reviewed it, and what happens next? | Analyst review status and notes, organizational policies with an owner, scoped exports and audit entries. | A risk score prioritizes review. A policy label records intent. Apply blocking, prompt DLP and incident response through the systems responsible for those controls. |

In the demo, compare the **Perplexity** DNS observation with the **Ollama** endpoint signal. Inspect the **Microsoft Copilot** approval example (target `catalog-3`) in **Governance**. Record one source limitation, one evidence-supported conclusion and one open question. Demo findings and decisions are synthetic.

## Design an isolated pilot

Name a CIO or CISO sponsor, an operational owner and a privacy reviewer. Set an end date, an approved collection purpose and a documented cohort such as one DNS source and a small group of managed test endpoints. Set the acceptable reporting delay, baseline and load envelope before collecting. Installing the server starts the platform. An operator must configure, enroll and activate each collection source.

| Gate | Owner | Evidence to attach before expanding the pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Source and attribution | Network and endpoint leads | Feed one known catalog-matching DNS observation and inventory one known endpoint tool. Confirm the expected source, timestamps and interpretation for both. Record collection points and exclusions in the pilot record and verify observations in **Sources & coverage**. Verify that a deliberately excluded test source remains outside the claimed coverage. [Source guide](collectors.md) · [Network evidence](network-analysis.md) |
| Review and access | SOC lead and identity owner | Have an analyst record a test finding's review status and reason. Verify the decision in **Discoveries** and review its audit evidence with an administrator. Verify that an authorized analyst can perform a scoped export and that a viewer is denied the same export and write. Document access and distribution rules for exports. [SSO and SCIM acceptance](sso-scim.md#pilot-acceptance) |
| Data scope and location | Privacy and platform owners | Approve allowed data fields, retention and permitted locations for stores, collector spools, logs, backups and exports. Inspect a test event and exercise identity expiry and purge on isolated data. Record external connections and transfers. Pseudonymization applies to new server processing and leaves earlier data and local spools to their own controls. [Data flow](architecture.md) · [Retention and pseudonymization](collector-operations.md#retain-and-pseudonymize-identities) |
| Restore and rollback | Platform owner | In an isolated environment, rehearse restore of the required stores, configuration and keys, then verify ingestion and console access. Record measured recovery time and data loss against the agreed targets. Rehearse the rollback procedure for the pinned revision and its compatible schemas. [Backup and restore](deployment.md#backup-and-restore) · [Upgrade qualification](production-qualification.md#startup-and-upgrade) |
| Target qualification | Platform and identity owners | Pin the source commit, configuration revision and image digests. Record CI and retained build evidence for that revision. Qualify the actual OS and fleet, Microsoft tenant and roles, target Kubernetes cluster and ingress if used, and peak event rate, queue recovery and reporting latency. [CI runs](https://github.com/alebgl77/open-shadow-ai/actions/workflows/ci.yml) · [Delivery evidence](production-delivery.md) · [Real installation](production-qualification.md#real-installation) |

**Go** when each applicable gate has an owner, dated evidence and sponsor acceptance. Keep the pilot isolated and time-limited while a gate is unresolved. **No-go** for expansion if provenance is misleading, access or retention fails, location and transfers remain unresolved, or recovery falls outside the agreed targets. Retain only the approved evidence for the decision and record an owner and due date for each open item.

## Review by jurisdiction

Use the same evaluation template in each deployment, then assess which laws, sector rules and voluntary guidance apply to the organization, its telemetry and the AI use cases it reviews. The platform supplies observations and review records for that assessment. The deploying organization determines applicability, completes the required assessments and signs off.

- **European Union and France.** For personal data, review purpose, minimization, retention, access and transfers under the GDPR, and determine whether a data protection impact assessment is required. For employee-related collection in France, verify necessity, proportionality and transparency with the privacy team. Assess AI Act obligations by system use, risk classification and operator role. Use the pilot's data-flow and coverage records as inputs to those reviews. [GDPR, Articles 5, 25, 32, 35 and Chapter V](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng) · [CNIL workplace guidance](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/controle-de-lactivite-des-personnes-employees) · [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689) · [European Commission implementation update](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force)
- **United States.** Use the voluntary NIST generative AI profile to structure inventory, provenance, responsibility and periodic review. Record which state and sector rules the deployment owner has assessed for the underlying use cases. [NIST AI 600-1, GV-1.5 and GV-1.6](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
- **Singapore.** Use IMDA governance guidance to discuss accountability and human oversight. For agentic systems, separately test approvals and limits on agent powers in the systems that enforce them. If the pilot processes personal data, review the separate PDPA protection, retention and transfer obligations. [IMDA AI governance frameworks](https://www.imda.gov.sg/about-imda/emerging-technologies-and-research/artificial-intelligence) · [PDPC key concepts](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Advisory-Guidelines/AG-on-Key-Concepts/Advisory-Guidelines-on-Key-Concepts-in-the-PDPA-17-May-2022.pdf)
- **Japan.** Use the METI/MIC AI Guidelines for Business to review who owns AI risk, how incidents are handled and when the organization reassesses its controls. Assign named owners and a review cadence to the pilot findings. [METI/MIC v1.2 guidelines and appendix](https://www.meti.go.jp/shingikai/mono_info_service/ai_shakai_jisso/20260331_report.html)

For installation and operations, follow the [implementation handbooks](README.md). The [security policy](../SECURITY.md) describes private vulnerability reporting.
